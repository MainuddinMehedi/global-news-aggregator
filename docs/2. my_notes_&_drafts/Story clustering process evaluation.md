**Rating: ~6/10 — workable for an MVP, but has two structural issues that will actively undermine the lifecycle design you've already built.**

**1. Momentum decay silently overrides your lifecycle config (most severe).** New clusters start at `momentumScore: 10`, decay by `-0.5` per hourly run, and get archived (`isActive: false`) at momentum ≤ 0. That's **zero new articles for ~20 hours = archived** — regardless of `impact`. Meanwhile `applyClusterLifecycle()` uses your carefully-tuned 7/10/21/35/60-day thresholds based on `impact`. For any story that doesn't get a new article roughly every 20 hours, momentum-decay archives it _before_ the day-based thresholds ever get a chance to apply. Your CRITICAL/HIGH lifecycle constants are effectively dead code for low-velocity stories — momentum decay always wins the race. Either tie momentum decay rate to `impact` (mirror the day-based config), or remove momentum-based archiving and let `applyClusterLifecycle()` be the single source of truth.

**2. Entity overlap is exact-string-match with no normalization.** "Vladimir Putin" / "Putin" / "President Putin" don't match each other. Two articles covering the same event with slightly different entity phrasing won't hit the ≥2-overlap threshold and will sit in separate (or no) groups. This was true with spaCy and stays true with any LLM unless you explicitly prompt for canonical entity names — worth adding to your entity-extraction prompt regardless of which model you land on.

**3. The 48h HOLDING window + 3-article critical mass structurally excludes "Slow Burn" stories** — which is one of the five lifecycle statuses you document. An entity-overlap group of 1-2 articles gets released, then archived as `ARCHIVED_UNCLUSTERED` after 48h and **never reconsidered**. If a slow-developing story accrues articles at <3 per 48h window across multiple runs, it never reaches critical mass and never clusters, even after a week of cumulative coverage. Either extend the holding window for low-volume-but-recurring entity combos, or lower critical mass for entities tied to your gazetteer's high-weight terms (geopolitically significant by definition).

**4. Grouping is anchor-centric, not true connected-components.** Group formation only checks new articles against article _i_'s entities, not the group's cumulative entity set. Order-dependent — B and C might belong together but never group if neither overlaps ≥2 with whichever article happens to be "i" first. Minor compared to #1/#3, but worth a pass once the bigger items are fixed.

**5. No run-level dedup across separately-formed groups.** If the same real-world story splits into two entity-overlap groups that don't share entities with each other (different angle, different named actors), each could independently create a "new cluster" in the same run — relying entirely on the LLM's "avoid duplicates" instruction with only the _other_ group's cluster as context (since `activeClusters.unshift` only updates after each chunk). Low probability, but worth a post-run dedup pass on newly-created clusters with high title/summary similarity.

**Priority order:** fix #1 first (it's actively breaking a feature you already built), then #2 (compounds with your new LLM-based entity extraction — richer entities won't help if matching stays exact-string), then #3 if Slow Burn stories matter to your use case.
